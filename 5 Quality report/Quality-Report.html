Visual reviews: what was found and fixed

Round 1 · Room tool, 56 screenshots of the phone, projector and console with 22 test people

Found
  • Inputs in the phone's pop-up sheets had no styling
  • "1 dots left" grammar on the dot counter
  • Projector text too small for the back of a room
  • Game answers did not stack cleanly on the projector
  • The findings wall showed too many cards to read
  • A finished game round could be reopened by mistake
  • Opening a second activity left the first one taking answers
Fixed
  • Styled sheet inputs
  • Counter says dot or dots
  • Projector text and bars enlarged
  • Answers stack under the question
  • Wall capped at 9 to 12 cards
  • Reopening a finished round is refused, with a test
  • Showing another activity closes the first, with a test

Round 2 · All 207 slides rendered and reviewed as images

Found
  • Hub labels wrapped onto three lines
  • "Choose a 4-digit PIN" crowded its card
  • The small ladder on session dividers was unreadable
  • Timeline labels cramped in short steps
  • Answer text ran into the tick on the outcome answers slide
  • Long option headers wrapped into the slide title
  • Placeholder text "Best when: Default." on eight options
  • Understanding steps numbered from 0 on some slides, from 1 on others
  • Arrows crossed the labels on the hand-over map
  • Every boundary slide claimed the design proposes option A; for boundary 3 it proposes C
  • The thank-you slide sat before the last session's options
  • The Northgate record said the deal was signed before it entered contract drafting
  • Three words appeared before the session that explains them
  • The deck file was 12.4 MB because images were stored many times
Fixed
  • Shorter hub labels
  • Shorter step text
  • Replaced with eight progress dots and "Step n of 8"
  • Smaller labels in narrow steps
  • Text box narrowed
  • Short session names in option headers
  • Real "best when" lines written for all eight
  • Steps numbered 1 to 8 everywhere: slides, phones, print, guide
  • Map redrawn with one straight arrow per hand-over
  • Each boundary marks the option its design proposes
  • Thank-you is now the last slide of Day 2
  • Dates corrected to 8 and 29 September
  • Rephrased in plain words
  • One copy per image: 2.6 MB, same slides

Round 5 · Independent review against the brief: 49 findings (1 blocker, 19 major, 29 minor)

Found
  • Northgate was signed on 29 September yet shown as in contract drafting; a stage date fell after the workshop
  • Session dividers named another session's step; a second step numbering competed with the ladder
  • Record and reality appeared together, so the room could not guess first
  • Build-team placeholders and 'Type here' would project if left empty
  • Day 1 said the releasing manager approves a release; Day 2 said it was open
  • 'Held', 'the design' and engine names were used without being explained
  • Words like happy path, bug bounty, backlog, pre-mortem, median and unlock on participant screens
  • Check answers could be guessed: usually the longest option, mostly B; boundary slides showed the proposal first
  • The outcome check's right answer failed the session's own test; the boundaries check tested a footnote
  • No time slot for the Day 2 ladder; Northgate disappeared after the first Day 2 session
  • Dense slides: twelve done checks as text, small fill-in tables, tool screenshots with tiny labels
  • Seat cards folded at the wrong edge and two of eight seats were missing; four promised paper versions were not printed
  • The room screen showed full names in scores; a finished timer stayed on screen; 'None of them' could be ticked with others
Fixed
  • Northgate is last year's deal, replayed; Day 2 runs it again; every date is consistent
  • Dividers say 'Still at step n' when a session does not move the room up; step 8 is the close; the engine map lost its own step labels
  • Each stage is two slides: the record and a question, then the answer and the lesson
  • Every placeholder slide reads well with nothing added; no ghost text
  • Day 1's answer is 'a named person, looking at the evidence'; Day 2 settles which person
  • Held is defined at 11:00; 'the draft' and 'the new way of working' replace 'the design'; engine names stay in the notes and appendix
  • Plain words throughout: Everything goes right, Problem hunt, to-do list, Imagine it failed, middle answer, bend
  • Options of equal length, answers spread over A, B and C (the spec checker now refuses guessable checks); boundary slides are neutral
  • The outcome check uses the full sentence with a start and a record; the boundaries check asks what happens to a dispute
  • Three minutes for the ladder in every close option, a steps 1–8 phone poll, and a poster for both days; Northgate on every boundary, the rotation bridge, the live run and a before-and-after slide
  • Done checks as four tiles; tables of four columns at 15–16 pt; the chain and the stuck job redrawn as diagrams
  • Fold line in the middle and eight seat cards; round cards, quiz cards, a stuck-job card, the role list and exception slips added
  • Scores show first name and initial, and the join screen says so; the timer clears itself; 'None of them' stands alone

Round 6 · Second independent check of every fix: 37 of 49 fixed, 11 partly, 1 not, and 11 new problems

Found
  • The 'last year' framing was missing from the guide, the example sheet and the decision cards
  • Slide 200 and seat card 4 still named the releasing manager, although Day 2 settles who approves a release
  • The 15:15 prediction poll labelled its rounds 'Step' and compared two different questions
  • Three check answers were still the longest option; one repeated the answer slide word for word; one marked the draft rule right while the room could replace it
  • The model outcome on slide 52 had no start; the end points B and C sounded the same
  • 'Every Mac has Python' is no longer true; the guide missed the right-click tip
  • The wheel's numbers ended upside down and the spin did not animate; the findings wall cut off a row
  • Rule court always put the draft as Rule 1; decision card 5 said the release was refused
  • The appendix glossaries were text only; 'pilot' was used before it was explained; small wording traces
Fixed
  • Shown on the example sheet, the decision cards ('Northgate, last year') and in the guide
  • Slide 200 says 'a named person, with the evidence'; seat cards show the decision and a line to write in the role the room agreed
  • Rounds are 'Prediction 1, 2…' and each stands alone
  • No check answer is longer than the others by more than 10%; the outcome check is a paraphrase; the rules check asks which rule is complete. The spec checker enforces all three
  • Slide 52 starts 'From the day a deal is real'; B now says signing is not checked, C that the deal cannot be signed until the plan is approved; slide 58 says the build is made to C
  • Guide and README say how to check for Python and where to get it; the tip is in both
  • The wheel spins from its last position and ends with the numbers upright; the findings wall shows six cards
  • Rule 1 and Rule 2 alternate; card 5 says nobody asked
  • Three appendix glossary slides with an icon per word; 'Pilot' is defined at 11:45 on Day 2, before first use; footers and notes plain

Still needs a person

  • Slides were checked as images made without PowerPoint, and the brand title font was replaced by Arial in those images. Open the deck once in PowerPoint on the presenting laptop and page through it. Titles were given room to spare.
  • The deck comes with the recommended mix: the other options and the 'Three ways' menus are hidden. If you change a session's option, the guide lists the slides to show and hide.
  • The slide-to-slide links are checked in the file. Click the Hub and Options links once in slide show mode.
  • Test the room tool on the venue Wi-Fi with two phones the day before, and check that the laptop has Python 3. The room tool README has plans B, C and D.
  • The fill-in-live slides are blank on purpose: the facilitator types the room's agreements into them during the session.
  • The sponsor's real case (Welcome, option C) and participants' own cases come from the room, so they are not in the kit.
  • 'Top of license' keeps the client's own spelling, as the name of the engine and the planning method.
  • The guide quotes each session's agenda line as printed, including words the slides avoid, such as 'completion conditions'.
  • The two appendix images (the engine map and the planning method chart) are screenshots; open the reference pages for detail. The method chart is in AED, as in the method page.
  • Print quantities depend on how many people come. The guide's Print kit tab gives quantities per table or per person.
