Naming the risk correctly

Two measures, not "value at risk"

"Value at risk" has a precise meaning in finance: a loss level that will not be exceeded with a stated confidence 19. Our earlier sketches used the term for an average, which a finance reader would challenge. The method now reports two measures, named as in the planning policy draft.

Expected value of work at risk

The average over all scenarios. The plan's main goal.

Σ roles to fill: chance won × value lost if late
  + chance lost × cost wasted by preparing
+ Σ accounts left: value at risk when a key person
  leaves without a same-skill backfill

Bad case, 90%

The average of the worst 10% of scenarios. The bad case decision makers weigh.

mean of the worst 10% of scenario losses
(CVaR at 90%, Rockafellar and Uryasev)

We do not report the plain percentile: percentile measures can mislead when added across deals 20. The 90% level is our choice; banks' 97.5% is a regulatory rule, not a guide for staffing 21.

Worked live in this page

Prepare now, or wait for the signature?

Preparing early means starting a release, a chain or a skill-up before the deal is won. It costs something if the deal is lost. Waiting costs value if the deal is won and the right person cannot be freed in time. Two-stage stochastic programming gives the rule for each role 13:

chance won × value saved by preparing>chance lost × cost wasted by preparing

Invented roles. Stage chances are the PoC planner's workshop placeholders. A role won without preparation has month from signature to start to free its person. Values in thousands of AED.

What the evidence supports

Supported, and still to prove

Supported by published work

✓Chains of internal moves are a long-studied way vacancies travel through organisations 78.
✓Promoted workers outperformed external hires for two years and left less, while hires were paid about 18% more, in one bank 10.
✓Stage-weighted pipeline and tentative bookings are standard tool practice 13.
✓Scenario planning, integer programming and CVaR are standard, well-documented methods 131519.

Not proven, or a caution

!"Top of license" is loosely defined even in healthcare, and its outcomes are rarely measured 6. Here it is an analogy, made precise by the firm's levels.
!Stage chances in CRM tools are defaults, not calibrated forecasts 12. They must be tested on the firm's own closed deals.
!Internal hires chosen by open posting outperformed those slotted in by managers 12. A planner proposing names is close to slotting, so opt-in and open posting stay in.
!Workers promoted and transferred at the same time did worse than other internal movers 10. Moves that change both level and client are flagged.
!No published study shows planned chains beat one-at-a-time filling. The firm's own results must show it.
What this means for the PoC planner

Five corrections to the planner already built

The PoC planner uses the right family of methods: a constraint solver, a month-by-month horizon and an ordered set of goals. Measured against the sources, five things needed changing. The first is adopted; the other four are with the team building it.

Rename its "value at risk" adoptedIt computes deal value × role share × stage chance for each unstaffed role. The planning policy draft v0.2 now calls this the expected value of work at risk, not value at risk.
Add scenarios and the tail measureIt scores one weighted outcome. Sampling which deals win would give the tail measure and show the bad case 1419.
Charge for preparing earlyActing on a deal that is then lost costs nothing in the model today, so the prepare-or-wait rule above cannot work.
Make "same input, same plan" hold under a time limitOne worker and a fixed seed repeat, but a wall-clock time limit does not. Use the solver's deterministic time limit, pin its version, record the model fingerprint, and say "optimal" only when the solver proves it 18.
Calibrate the stage chancesThey are workshop placeholders. Score them on closed deals with the Brier score before relying on them 2324.
